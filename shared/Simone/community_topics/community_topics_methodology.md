# Community topic coding — exploratory, AI-assisted

## Corpus and procedure
- Source: community_topics.zip; SHA-256: 82df392891c43518e7abc3c3f3cf3470acb7c77d3692d16654249bebedfe0286.
- 97 pairs / 194 chats; 27,386 sampled messages; 1,104,057 eligible messages. Archive manifest specifies seed 42, chronological rank-stratified sampling, max 100 channel / 200 group messages, 1,200-character export limit, no selection by toxicity. Counts and identifiers checked against the previously supplied plan.json. 1,395 samples were shortened.
- Coding was performed at chat level, separately for channel and group, using TF-IDF terms (unigrams and bigrams) across the 194 chat samples and qualitative inspection of excerpts. This is an exploratory assessment, NOT manual annotation of every sampled message or a measured topic distribution. No toxicity values were used in the coding step, but prior conversation included the earlier results and community classifications, so this should not be represented as independently blinded annotation.
- Taxonomy: Politics; Health & Medicine; Geopolitics & War; Conspiracy theories; Alternative science & history; Religion & Spirituality; Extremism & Radicalism; Economy & Finance; Lifestyle & Community; Entertainment & Culture; Mixed / Other; Insufficient evidence. Entertainment & Culture and Insufficient evidence were added to the previously proposed taxonomy. Primary labels are qualitative assessments; secondary labels are nonexclusive. Extremism refers to sampled ideological content, not membership or individuals. Commercial promotions are qualitative flags, not verified spam or fraud. Unflagged does not mean absent. A finance label can reflect promotional activity rather than genuine discussion.
- Observed languages are qualitative, not automated detection, dominant-language estimates or nationality. Geographic focus refers only to the subject of content; unknown where weak. Country labels must not be interpreted as nationality/residence. Confidence is a review-priority heuristic: low if <25 informative messages, mixed/insufficient/extremism label or promotion flag; medium otherwise. No high-confidence claims; ALL labels need human review. Informative text heuristic = at least 25 alphabetic characters after removal of recognized redaction markers. It is not a semantic measure.
- Both labels and comparison are exploratory. Different primary labels do not establish a topic shift over time. Mixed or insufficient labels are excluded from the same/different comparison. Use the channel label as a fixed pair-level baseline if comparing paired toxicity by topic; sensitivity analysis should consider mismatched groups and possible promotional contamination. Avoid ranking topics with small n, and validate labels with two independent annotators.
- community_topics_toxicity_partial.csv joins the fixed channel label to the 42 completed pairs in the previously supplied pair_statistics.csv, using chunks, threshold 0.5, category toxicity. The output is descriptive, not confirmatory; the 42 processed pairs are a nonrandom subset, and the model has not yet been human-validated or calibrated across languages.
- No raw message text or sender IDs are exported. Real community names and pair IDs were restored from plan.json only after coding. Keep data and results in approved private storage; don't publish identifiable chat labels without approval.
